# Portfolio Case Study: Decentralized Remote Care Transformation
**Author:** Brianna Holmes, Senior Strategist Candidate  
**Target Organization:** Nova Scotia Department of Health and Wellness (DHW)  
**Project Type:** Strategic Business Case & Health Technology Assessment (HTA) Framework

## Executive Summary
This strategic business case outlines a **$2.02M three-year capital investment** to deploy a low-carbon, decentralized remote patient monitoring (RPM) network across Nova Scotia’s Northern, Western, and Eastern health zones. Targeting high-risk chronic disease cohorts (**Diabetes, COPD, and CHF**), the model transitions care from reactive hospital environments directly into patient homes. By optimizing legacy software architectures and maximizing nursing scope, the framework addresses acute system capacity constraints, drives regional health equity, and aligns with provincial green infrastructure mandates.

---

## Strategic Pillars & Framework Value

### 1. Clinical Efficiency & Capacity Optimization
The model establishes a regional triage hub powered by a synchronized, nurse-driven workforce operating at the top of their clinical scope:
*   **LPNs:** Monitor asynchronous telemetry ingestion streams (glucose, spirometry, daily weights) and manage home device logistics.
*   **RNs:** Act as gatekeepers to evaluate compounding clinical indicators and execute virtual care coaching.
*   **NPs:** Function as autonomous clinical leads to interpret longitudinal trends, titrate medications, and authorize home-based interventions.
*   **Impact:** Projects a **22% reduction in avoidable emergency department presentations** and a **15% decrease in acute inpatient length of stay (LOS)**.

### 2. Fiscal Accountability & Tech Asset-Light Architecture
Leveraging a zero-dollar marginal software footprint strategy, the initiative achieves severe cost containment by anchoring all data pipelines within existing provincial infrastructure:
*   **One Person One Record (OPOR):** Native integration for all clinical charting and biometric telemetry.
*   **Microsoft 365 / Teams for Healthcare:** Utilized for secure, virtual patient consultations and cross-zone clinical huddles.
*   **Labor Economics:** Staffing models are mathematically grounded using accurate hourly wage baselines from the Nova Scotia Nurses' Union (NSNU) collective agreement.

### 3. Sustainability & Environmental Infrastructure
*   **Carbon Offset:** Eliminates **140 metric tons of patient-travel carbon emissions annually by Year 3**, scaling proportionally to **1,400 metric tons annually by Year 10**.
*   **Procurement Mandates:** Prioritizes energy-efficient corporate hardware and vendor supply chains that actively eliminate single-use medical plastics.

### Key Scaling Milestones
*   **Year 1:** Establishes the 6-nurse hub team and initiates tracking for 450 high-risk patients, diverting ~45 complex hospitalizations.
*   **Year 2:** Paces regional operations safely, allowing workflows to fully stabilize over an initial four-year pilot runway.
*   **Year 5:** Expands to 3 decentralized regional hubs (1 per rural health zone), freeing up **1,560 physical hospital bed days** annually.
*   **Year 10:** Full provincial integration. Advanced automated alerting algorithms optimize nurse-to-patient capacities, allowing a tight **$3.5M operational budget** to manage a **$100.2M system risk pool**. This saves **$31.57M in annual expenditures** and completely eliminates the need for **$14.5M in physical hospital brick-and-mortar expansions**.

## Risk Mitigation Framework
To ensure strict clinical safety, public asset preservation, and absolute compliance with the *Personal Health Information Act (PHIA)* of Nova Scotia, four targeted mitigation layers are integrated natively into the design:
1.  **Infrastructure Gaps:** Deploys carrier-agnostic, dual-SIM LTE biometric modules with fault-tolerant, edge-cached asynchronous data buffering to bypass rural broadband limitations.
2.  **Data Security:** Enforces end-to-end AES-256 encryption wrapped in a mandatory, hardware-enforced provincial VPN layer. Absolutely no clinical data is stored on local patient hardware; entries write directly into the audited OPOR core architecture.
3.  **Frontline Burnout:** Leverages multi-tiered baseline thresholds script-side to filter minor, localized data fluctuations into patient self-management templates, preserving active nurse huddle capacity exclusively for severe comorbidity alarms.
4.  **Supply Chain Stability:** Formulates a multi-vendor pooling arrangement to bypass global tracking device backlogs while holding a secure 15% physical hardware buffer stock at regional hubs.
